How it works

All pressure conversions route through the pascal (Pa = N/m²), the SI unit. One atmosphere (atm) at sea level = 101,325 Pa = 1.01325 bar = 14.696 psi. The bar (100,000 Pa ≈ 0.987 atm) is widely used in engineering because it's close to one atmosphere.

PSI (pounds per square inch) dominates in the US for tires, plumbing, and industrial pressure. mmHg (millimeters of mercury, also called torr after Torricelli) is used in medicine and vacuum systems — 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr.

Gauge pressure vs absolute pressure: tire gauges read gauge pressure (pressure above atmospheric). Absolute = gauge + 1 atm. 32 psi gauge ≈ 46.7 psi absolute ≈ 2.21 atm ≈ 224 kPa.

Real-world applications

Bicycle tires: Road bikes use 80-120 psi (5.5-8.3 bar = 550-830 kPa). Mountain bikes use 30-50 psi (2-3.5 bar). Under-inflation causes pinch flats; over-inflation causes harsh rides.

Scuba diving: Every 10 meters underwater adds 1 bar (14.5 psi = 100 kPa). At 30 meters, you experience 4 bar absolute. Your air tank is filled to about 200 bar when full.

Blood pressure: Normal systolic is 90-120 mmHg (12-16 kPa = 1.7-2.3 psi). Healthy reading is 120/80 mmHg.

How pressure units relate

1 atm = 14.696 psi = 1.01325 bar = 101.325 kPa = 760 mmHg = 760 torr. Pressure is force per area. Bar = 100 kPa, almost one atmosphere.

Frequently asked questions

What is normal atmospheric pressure?

1 atm = 14.696 psi = 1.01325 bar = 101.325 kPa = 760 mmHg. Denver at 1 mile elevation has about 83% of this pressure.

Tire pressure - gauge or absolute?

Tire gauges read gauge pressure (pressure above atmospheric). 32 psi gauge is about 46.7 psi absolute. Always inflate to the manufacturer recommended gauge pressure.

Why do bar and atm differ?

Bar = 100 kPa exactly (metric engineering unit). Atm = 101.325 kPa (sea-level air pressure). They differ by about 1.3%. Most European pressure gauges use bar.
