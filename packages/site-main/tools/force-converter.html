How it works

All force conversions route through the newton (N), the SI unit. 1 N is the force needed to accelerate 1 kilogram at 1 m/s². The pound-force (lbf or lbF) is the English unit — 1 lbf ≈ 4.4482 N. Do not confuse lbf with lb (pound-mass): on Earth's surface, 1 lbf ≈ 1 lb of mass, but they are fundamentally different quantities.

The kilogram-force (kgf, also called kilopond, kp) is a metric unit still used informally: 1 kgf = 9.80665 N — it's the weight of 1 kg under standard gravity. A 70 kg person exerts about 686 N (70.0 kgf, 154 lbf) on the ground.

Dynes are the CGS unit (1 dyn = 10⁻⁵ N), mostly seen in physics textbooks now. Tons-force (tf) is sometimes used for heavy machinery: 1 tf = 9,806.65 N ≈ 2,205 lbf.

Real-world applications

Weightlifting: A 100 kg squat produces about 981 N of force on your legs. On the Moon, the same 100 kg mass produces only 162 N (1/6 of Earth's gravity).

Car accidents: A 70 kg driver decelerating from 100 km/h to 0 in 0.5 seconds experiences about 3,890 N of force - nearly 4 g's.

Mass vs force

Newton = SI unit of force. Pound-force = English unit of force. A 1 kg mass exerts 9.80665 N on Earth. US scales measure pounds-force but colloquially use it as mass. Physics: F = ma.

Frequently asked questions

Newton vs pound-force - what is the difference?

1 N is about 0.2248 lbf, and 1 lbf is about 4.448 N. On Earth, 1 kg mass weighs 9.807 N (about 2.205 lbf). Do not confuse lbf (force) with lb (mass).

How much force does gravity exert on me?

Force equals mass times gravity. A 70 kg person experiences about 686 N (70 kgf, 154 lbf) of gravitational force. In space with microgravity, this drops to nearly zero.

What is a dyne?

The CGS unit: 1 dyn = 10 to the negative 5 N. Mostly seen in old physics papers. Modern SI universally uses newtons.
