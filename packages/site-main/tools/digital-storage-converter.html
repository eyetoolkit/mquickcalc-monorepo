How it works

Storage capacity is famously confusing because the industry uses two different prefix systems. Decimal (SI) prefixes are powers of 1,000: 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes. Binary (IEC) prefixes are powers of 1,024: 1 MiB = 1,048,576 bytes. Hard drives advertise in decimal; operating systems report in binary — that's why a "1 TB" drive shows as about 931 GB in Windows.

The conversion factor between them is 1,024 / 1,000 = 1.024 at every step. So 1 decimal gigabyte ≈ 0.931 binary gibibyte. This tool shows both columns so you can see exactly where the number comes from.

Smaller units are unambiguous (KB, MB work fine), but once you hit GB vs GiB, TB vs TiB, and PB vs PiB, the difference compounds. That "16 GB RAM" in your laptop is actually 16 GiB (17.18 billion bytes), while your 500 GB SSD is decimal (500,000,000,000 bytes).

Real-world applications

Photo library: Typical phone photo = 3-5 MB. 128 GB phone stores about 25,000 photos.

Why 1 TB = 931 GB

Decimal: 1 TB = 10¹² bytes. Binary: 1 TiB = 2⁴⁰ bytes. The ratio: 1 TB ÷ 1,099,511,627,776 = 0.9095 × 1024 = 931.32 GiB. The gap grows with scale: at the PB level, it's over 13%.

Why does my 1 TB drive show 931 GB?

Drive makers use decimal (1 TB = 10¹² bytes). Your OS uses binary (1 TiB = 1,099,511,627,776 bytes). The ratio is about 0.9095, so 1 TB decimal = 931.32 binary GB.

Why two prefix systems?

Decimal prefixes (kB, MB, GB) follow ISO 1000-standard. Binary (KiB, MiB, GiB) was standardized by the IEC in 1998 for base-2. Windows stubbornly uses decimal abbreviations for binary values.

Do I really need to care?

Solid-state drive shoppers: binary. Cloud storage (AWS S3, Google Drive): decimal (they round up, so you lose less). Networking: decimal (Gbps = 10⁹ bits/s).

Frequently asked questions

Why does my 1 TB drive show as 931 GB?

Hard drives use decimal (1 TB = 1,000,000,000,000 bytes) but Windows/macOS use binary (1 TiB = 1,099,511,627,776 bytes). 1 trillion divided by 1.099 trillion = 0.931.

Is RAM measured in GB or GiB?

Computer RAM uses binary (actual power-of-2 addressing), so 16 GB RAM is really 16 GiB = 17,179,869,184 bytes. The industry just says GB colloquially.

What is the biggest storage unit?

Yottabyte (YB) = 10 to the 24 bytes decimal, yobibyte (YiB) = 2 to the 80 bytes binary. No single device reaches this yet - total global data is in the zettabyte range.
