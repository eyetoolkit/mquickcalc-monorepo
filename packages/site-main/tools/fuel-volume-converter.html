How it works

Fuel volume conversions route through the liter. The main pitfall is gallons: there are two sizes. The US liquid gallon is 3.7854 L; the UK (imperial) gallon is 4.5461 L — about 20% larger. Canadian gas stations switched to liters decades ago but you'll still see prices labeled "per liter" or occasionally "per 100 km".

Quarts and pints follow the same pattern: a US quart = 0.9464 L, a UK quart = 1.1365 L. A barrel of oil (42 US gallons = 158.987 L) is the global standard for crude pricing.

This tool distinguishes US and UK at every step. If you're comparing fuel prices across countries, make sure both sides use the same gallon — it changes the math dramatically.

Real-world applications

Gas station economics: US stations price per gallon (3.785 L); EU stations per liter. USD 3.00/gallon is USD 0.793/L. GBP 1.50/L is GBP 5.68/gallon.

How fuel volume units differ

US and UK both use gallons/quarts/pints/fluid ounces — but they are all different sizes. An imperial gallon is 20% larger than a US gallon. Metric is consistent (1 L = 1000 mL). Mixing imperial systems creates errors of up to 25%.

Frequently asked questions

Why are US and UK gallons different?

The US liquid gallon was based on wine measurement (3.785 L); the UK imperial gallon on beer (4.546 L). A UK gallon is 20% larger. US dry gallon is yet another size - 4.405 L.

How many liters in a barrel of oil?

One standard oil barrel = 42 US gallons = 158.987 liters. This is the global pricing benchmark for crude oil.

Which gallon do Canadian gas stations use?

Canada switched to liters for fuel pricing in the 1970s. Prices are per liter, but many older signs still show per-gallon equivalents using the US gallon.
