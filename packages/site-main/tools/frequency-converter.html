How it works

Frequency conversions route through hertz (Hz = 1 cycle per second). Rotations per minute (RPM) convert to Hz by dividing by 60: 60 RPM = 1 Hz, 3,600 RPM = 60 Hz (typical car engine at cruising speed). To go the other way, multiply Hz by 60.

Kilohertz (kHz), megahertz (MHz), and gigahertz (GHz) are simply powers of 1,000: 1 GHz = 1,000 MHz = 1,000,000 kHz = 1,000,000,000 Hz. This tool also handles angular frequency in radians per second (rad/s): multiply Hz by 2π, so 60 Hz ≈ 376.99 rad/s.

The unit circle gives the relationship: 1 Hz = 2π rad/s = 60 RPM. You'll see all three in audio (Hz), mechanics (RPM), and signal processing (rad/s).

Real-world applications

Car engines: A typical sedan redlines at 6,500 RPM = 108 Hz. Formula 1 cars rev to 15,000 RPM = 250 Hz. Electric motors can spin 20,000 RPM = 333 Hz.

Music and audio: Standard tuning A = 440 Hz. Middle C = 261.63 Hz. Human hearing range is roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

How frequency units relate

1 Hz = 1 cycle per second = 60 RPM = 2π radians/second. Period = 1/frequency. Audio Hz = sound vibration. 50 Hz EU, 60 Hz US power grids.

Frequently asked questions

How many RPM is 60 Hz?

60 Hz = 3,600 RPM (60 times 60). This is why electric motors designed for 60 Hz power spin at multiples of 3,600 RPM. 50 Hz regions use 3,000 RPM.

What is the audible frequency range?

Humans hear roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Dog whistles go up to 40 kHz. Ultrasound medical imaging uses 2-18 MHz.

GHz vs MHz - which is bigger?

1 GHz = 1,000 MHz = 1,000,000 kHz = 1,000,000,000 Hz. Wi-Fi uses 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands; FM radio is 88-108 MHz.
