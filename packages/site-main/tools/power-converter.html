How it works

All power conversions route through the watt (W = 1 joule per second). The key distinction is between mechanical horsepower (1 hp = 745.7 W) and metric horsepower (1 PS = 735.5 W), sometimes called "pony power" or "CV" in Europe. They differ by about 1.4%.

Kilowatts (kW) are standard in the US for car engines now, but Europe still uses PS/hp interchangeably. One kW ≈ 1.341 hp (mech) ≈ 1.360 PS (metric). Electric motors are rated in kW; IC engines in hp or PS depending on region.

BTU/h and calories/min are thermal power units. 1 kW ≈ 3,412 BTU/h ≈ 143.3 cal/min. Furnaces and air conditioners use BTU/h; industrial heating may use kW.

Real-world applications

Car engine output: A Toyota Camry makes 203 hp (mechanical) = 206 PS (metric) = 151 kW. European ads use PS; US ads use hp. The difference is tiny but kW is 32% lower.

Solar panels: A typical home system is 5-10 kW peak. A 6 kW system generates about 7,200 kWh/year. A single panel is 300-400 W. 1 kW peak = 1.34 hp.

Watt vs horsepower

1 mechanical hp = 745.7 W. 1 metric hp (PS) = 735.5 W. James Watt coined the term in 1775 as a marketing number — roughly how much work a horse could do per hour. Two conflicting definitions persist.

Frequently asked questions

Mechanical hp vs metric PS - which for my car?

Mechanical horsepower (hp, SAE) = 745.7 W. Metric PS (CV, DIN) = 735.5 W. US car ads use hp; European ads use PS. A 150 hp car is about 152 PS or 112 kW.

How many watts does my PC use?

Desktop PCs: 300-600 W. Gaming rigs: 700-1000+ W. Laptops: 30-80 W. Multiply watts by hours used to get kWh for your electricity bill.

BTU/h for furnaces - what size?

Furnace output is rated in BTU/h. A 2,000 sq ft home typically needs 60,000-100,000 BTU/h. 1 kW is about 3,412 BTU/h.
