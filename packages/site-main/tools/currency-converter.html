How it works

Every conversion on this page runs through one base currency: the US dollar. Each currency has a rate — how many units of it equal one US dollar — so converting between any two currencies is a single calculation: amount × (target rate ÷ source rate). There is no separate formula for each currency pair, because every rate is anchored to the same dollar.

The rates used here are mid-market rates, the midpoint between the buy and sell prices on the global currency market. That is the rate traders use with each other, and it is the fairest reference point for comparing what different providers charge. It is not the rate you will actually get: banks, card issuers and transfer services add a spread and sometimes fees on top, so the amount charged for a real transaction is usually a little worse than the rate shown here.

Rates are updated regularly from exchangerate-api.com. Because exchange rates move with markets, always check the live rate before making a large transfer or signing a contract in a foreign currency.

Real-world applications

Currency conversion is a daily necessity in online shopping, travel and cross-border business. When you buy from an overseas store, the price in your currency tells you what you will actually pay — and whether a bargain in dollars is still a bargain after conversion. Travelers use it to budget for trips and to spot unreasonable ATM or airport exchange rates.

Freelancers and sellers earning in foreign currencies need to know what a payment is worth in their home currency before accepting a rate. E-commerce sellers pricing products for multiple markets also rely on conversion to keep margins consistent across currencies. For anything beyond a quick check, compare against your bank or card provider's actual rate, since that is the number that will appear on your statement.

Frequently asked questions

How do currency conversions work?

Every currency has a rate relative to the US dollar, so conversion between any two currencies is a simple multiplication and division. Multiply your amount by the target currency's rate, then divide by the source currency's rate. For example, to find what 100 euros is in yen when 1 USD = 0.8667 EUR and 1 USD = 155.08 JPY, compute 100 × (155.08 ÷ 0.8667), which gives about 17,897 yen. This works in reverse for any currency pair without needing a separate table for each combination.

Are these the live exchange rates?

The rates shown are indicative mid-market rates sourced from exchangerate-api.com, last updated on 16 September 2026. They give you a fair reference for comparing conversions, but they are not a guarantee of the rate any provider will apply to your transaction. Exchange rates also move with markets throughout the day, so for large amounts, check the current rate before committing.

Why does my bank show a different rate?

Banks and payment processors add a spread on top of the mid-market rate to cover their costs and risk. The rate published here never includes that markup, so the amount actually charged on a card, ATM withdrawal or wire transfer is usually a little worse than what you see here. Some providers also charge a fixed fee per transaction, which makes the effective rate worse again for small amounts.

What are mid-market rates?

Mid-market rates are the midpoint between the buy and sell prices on the global currency market — the rate that traders exchange currencies at with each other. They are the fairest reference for comparing how much different providers charge, because every provider starts from the same mid-market number and then adds their own spread and fees on top.

Can I convert between any two currencies?

Yes. Because every currency in the table is anchored to the US dollar, any currency can be converted to any other in a single step — including currencies that rarely trade directly against each other, such as the Nigerian naira and the Thai baht.
