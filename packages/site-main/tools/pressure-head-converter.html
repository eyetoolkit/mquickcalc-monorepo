How it works

Pressure head is pressure expressed as the height of a water column. The formula is P = ρgh — density × gravity × height. For water at room temperature, 1 meter of water ≈ 9.80665 kPa ≈ 0.0967 atm ≈ 1.42 psi. Feet of water: 1 ft H₂O ≈ 2.989 kPa ≈ 0.433 psi.

This is essential for plumbing and hydraulics: a pump needs enough head to overcome the elevation difference plus friction losses. If your bathroom is 5 meters above the water tank, you need at least 49 kPa (0.48 atm, 7.1 psi) just to push water up there.

The conversion factor 1 m H₂O = 0.0981 bar = 9.80665 kPa is the one to memorize. Mercury is 13.6× denser than water, so 1 mmHg = 13.6 mmH₂O ≈ 133.3 Pa.

Real-world applications

Plumbing and water pressure: A 5-story building needs 40-50 meters of head (4-5 bar = 58-72 psi) at the top floor. Low-rise homes typically have 2-4 bar (29-58 psi) municipal pressure. Below 1.5 bar is considered low.

Well pump sizing: A well pump needs enough head to push water from the well bottom to your storage tank. A 50 m head pump (4.9 bar) handles most residential wells.

Hydrostatic pressure formula

P = ρgh. Water density ≈ 1000 kg/m³, gravity = 9.80665 m/s². 1 m of water = 0.098 bar. Mercury is 13.6× denser — that's why 760 mmHg = 1 atm = 10.33 m water.

Frequently asked questions

How many psi is 10 meters of water?

10 m H2O is about 14.2 psi, 0.981 bar, or 98.0665 kPa. Each meter of water column adds about 9.81 kPa of hydrostatic pressure.

Is pressure head the same as pressure?

Pressure head is pressure expressed as column height of a reference liquid (usually water for plumbing, mercury for vacuum). They measure the same physical quantity in different units.

Why does my well pump have head in specs?

Pump head is the maximum vertical distance the pump can push water. A 50 m head pump can overcome 50 m of elevation (about 4.9 bar or 71 psi) before water stops flowing.
