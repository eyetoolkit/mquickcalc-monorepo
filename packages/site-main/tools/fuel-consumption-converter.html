How fuel consumption works

Fuel economy has two opposite conventions. MPG-style measures distance per fuel — higher is better. L/100km measures fuel per distance — lower is better. They are mathematical reciprocals, so doubling your MPG halves your L/100km figure. The US gallon is 3.785 L but the UK imperial gallon is 4.546 L — that's why UK MPG numbers are always higher than US MPG for the same car.

Real-world applications

Real-world: 50 UK MPG = 41.7 US MPG (UK gallon is 20% larger). Winter: -10-20% MPG for gas cars, -20-40% EV range below -10 C. Tesla Model 3 = 200 Wh/km = 3.2 L/100km equivalent.

Frequently asked questions

Why does L/100km go down when MPG goes up?

They are inverse measures. MPG = distance divided by fuel. L/100km = fuel divided by distance. One is literally the reciprocal of the other (with unit conversions). Better fuel economy means higher MPG but lower L/100km.

US gallon vs UK gallon — what's the difference?

A US gallon is 3.785 liters. A UK imperial gallon is 4.546 liters — about 20% larger. That's why a UK-gallon MPG figure is higher than a US-gallon MPG for the same actual fuel economy.

What is a good fuel economy?

Below 6 L/100km (above 39 US MPG) is excellent. 6-8 L/100km is typical for modern sedans. Above 10 L/100km (below 24 MPG) is gas-guzzler territory. Electric vehicles use kWh/100km instead — roughly 6-8 kWh/100km is equivalent to about 29-39 L/100km gasoline.
