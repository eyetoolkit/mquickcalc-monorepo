How it works

Flow rate measures volume per time. All conversions route through cubic meters per second (m³/s). The US and UK use different gallons (US gal = 3.785 L, UK gal = 4.546 L), so gallons-per-minute conversion depends on which country's gallon you mean — this tool handles both.

Common plumbing units: liters per minute (L/min) for faucets, gallons per minute (GPM) for showerheads, and cubic feet per minute (CFM) for airflow in HVAC. 1 CFM ≈ 28.317 L/min, 1 US GPM ≈ 3.785 L/min.

For pumps and industrial flow, you'll see m³/h (cubic meters per hour) and m³/s. 1 m³/s = 3,600 m³/h = 211.9 CFM. The relationship is linear — just multiply or divide by the right factor.

Real-world applications

Plumbing and water supply: Standard showerheads flow 2.0-2.5 GPM (7.6-9.5 L/min). A low-flow model is 1.5 GPM. If you shower for 10 minutes, you use 15-25 gallons (57-95 L).

HVAC airflow: Bathroom exhaust fans are rated in CFM. A 50 CFM fan = 1,416 L/min. Air conditioners move 350-400 CFM per ton of cooling.

How flow rates relate

1 m³ = 1000 L = 35.3 ft³ = 264 US gal. Typical kitchen faucet: 5-6 L/min. Garden hose: 15-25 L/min. 4-inch water main: 500+ L/min.

Frequently asked questions

How many L/min is a good shower?

Standard showerheads are 2.0-2.5 GPM (about 7.6-9.5 L/min). Low-flow models are 1.5 GPM (5.7 L/min). Faucets are typically 2.0-3.0 GPM.

CFM vs L/min for air?

Cubic feet per minute (CFM) measures airflow for HVAC and ventilation. 1 CFM is about 28.3 L/min. A typical bathroom exhaust fan is 50-100 CFM.

US gallon vs UK gallon for flow?

Almost all flow rate specs use US gallons (3.785 L). UK gallons (4.546 L) are 20% larger. This tool handles both - check which your source means.
