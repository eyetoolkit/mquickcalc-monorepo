Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this car insurance estimate?

This calculator uses state-level average data from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners. Your actual premium depends on many factors we cannot verify: your exact credit score, previous insurance claims history, the specific year/make/model of your vehicle, your ZIP code's risk factors, and each carrier's underwriting rules. Use this as a budget range, not a quote.

Why does my state have higher average rates?

Insurance prices vary by state because each state has different regulations, minimum coverage requirements, average claim costs, fraud rates, and weather risks. For example, Florida's average is high due to hurricane risk, while Massachusetts is lower because state regulations limit certain rating factors.

Should I get full coverage or state minimum?

Full coverage (comprehensive + collision + liability) costs about 40% more than state minimum, but protects your own vehicle regardless of who's at fault. If your car is worth more than $5,000 or you have a loan/lease, full coverage is usually worth it.
