Frequently asked questions

What does renters insurance actually cover?

Three main things: (1) Personal property — your furniture, electronics, clothing, kitchenware if stolen/damaged. (2) Liability — if someone gets hurt in your apartment and sues you. (3) Additional living expenses — if your place is unlivable after a fire/flood, it pays for a hotel. It does NOT cover flood or earthquake — you need separate policies for those.

Do I really need renters insurance?

Yes, probably. Your landlord's insurance only covers the building structure, not your stuff. If a pipe bursts and ruins everything, that's your problem without coverage. And liability coverage protects you if a guest slips and falls. At $15-30/month, it's one of the cheapest forms of insurance you can buy.

How do I know how much coverage I need for my stuff?

Walk through each room and add up: cost of replacing your furniture, electronics (laptop, TV, phone), kitchen appliances, clothing (dozens of items!), bedding, and anything else you'd have to replace. Most people underestimate — start with $15,000 minimum for a one-bedroom.
