How mortgage amortization works

In the early years of a mortgage, most of your monthly payment goes toward interest — the bank is earning back the risk of lending you a large sum. As the principal balance shrinks, the interest portion of each payment decreases and the principal portion grows. This is why making extra principal payments early in a loan's life has an outsized effect on total interest paid.

The 20% equity date shows when your remaining balance drops to 80% of the original home value — the threshold at which you can typically cancel private mortgage insurance (PMI). If you put down less than 20%, lenders typically require PMI until you reach this point.
