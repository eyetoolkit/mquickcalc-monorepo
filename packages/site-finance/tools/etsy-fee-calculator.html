How Etsy seller fees work

Every time you make a sale on Etsy, the platform deducts a series of fees before you receive your payout. Understanding each one is essential before setting your prices — a 30% markup might sound healthy until you realize Etsy takes 12–15% of your gross, leaving you with a much thinner margin than expected. The fees fall into two categories: mandatory fees (transaction fee, payment processing, listing fee) that apply to every sale, and conditional fees (regulatory operating fee, off-site ad fee) that only apply in certain situations. This calculator lets you enter each fee type separately so you can model your exact net margin at any price point.

The transaction fee (currently 6.5% for most categories in the US) is charged on the item price plus any gift-wrapping charges — but not on the shipping fee, which is a common point of confusion. The payment processing fee (3% + $0.25 per order in the US) is charged on the item price plus shipping. Combined, these two fees alone take 9.5% + $0.25 from every order before you see a cent. The listing fee ($0.20 per listing) is charged when you publish or renew a listing — it does not expire automatically and you are charged each time you manually renew. If a listing renews automatically after 4 months, you are charged again. Regulatory operating fees (0.25% in the US, 0% in many other countries) are a relatively recent addition to Etsy's fee structure and vary by seller's location.

Understanding your take-home rate

Your take-home rate is the percentage of your gross sale price that you actually keep. At the default settings ($25 item, 6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 payment processing, $0.20 listing, no regulatory fee, no off-site ads): gross $25 minus $1.625 (transaction) minus $1.00 (payment processing) minus $0.25 (payment flat) minus $0.20 (listing) = $21.93 take-home, a take-home rate of 87.7%. With off-site ads at 12%, an attributed sale would lose an additional $3.00, dropping take-home to just 75.7%. This is why understanding which fees apply to your sales — and whether off-site ads are working for you — is essential for pricing decisions.

The off-site ads fee is the most misunderstood Etsy fee. Etsy automatically enrolls shops with fewer than $10,000 in sales in the off-site ads program. If a buyer clicks an Etsy ad and makes a purchase within 30 days (attributed via cookie), Etsy charges the off-site ad fee on that order. The fee is 12% for shops with less than $10,000 in annual sales, or 15% for shops above $10,000. Many sellers see this as an unwanted tax on traffic they did not ask for. However, if a sale through off-site ads generates net profit that exceeds what you would have earned with zero off-site ads (after the 12% fee), the program is working for you. Track which sales are attributed to off-site ads and calculate whether your net margin on those sales still exceeds your cost of goods.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Etsy take in total per sale?

At default settings ($25 item), mandatory fees total approximately $2.93 on a $25 sale — a take-home rate of approximately 88%. This consists of: 6.5% transaction fee ($1.63), 3% + $0.25 payment processing ($1.00 + $0.25), and $0.20 listing fee. With off-site ads enabled and a attributed sale, add another 12% ($3.00), bringing total fees to $5.93 — take-home drops to 76%. Etsy also charges a 2.5% currency conversion fee if you sell internationally and receive payment in a different currency than your payout account. Always use the calculator with your actual rates entered, not defaults.

Is the off-site ads fee negotiable or avoidable?

Shops that exceed $10,000 in annual sales can opt out of off-site ads entirely — this is the primary benefit of growing your Etsy revenue above that threshold. For shops still in the off-site ads program, you cannot negotiate the rate downward. However, you can reduce your effective off-site ads cost-per-attributed-sale by raising prices to maintain your target margin even on attributed sales. Many sellers simply raise all prices by 12–15% and offer a discount code for "direct" buyers as an incentive to find them through their own channels, effectively making off-site ads buyers pay a slight premium while loyal customers get a discount.

Why is my net different from what the calculator shows?

The most common reasons: (1) You are in a different country with different fee rates — Etsy fees vary significantly by seller location. (2) Your item is in a category with a higher transaction fee — some categories like Skin Care (9%) or Gemstone Jewelry (15%) have much higher referral fees. (3) Your buyer paid in a foreign currency — Etsy converts at a rate that includes a 2.5% spread. (4) You offered a discount or coupon — the transaction fee is calculated on the after-discount price. (5) Etsy Gift Wrapping or personalization charges are included in the transaction fee base. (6) You had a refund or cancellation — Etsy does not refund fees on refunded orders.

How do I price my items to earn a target profit?

Work backwards from your target margin. If your cost of goods is $8 and you want a 50% gross margin on a $25 selling price, your target net after Etsy fees is $12.50. Use the calculator to find the price that produces $12.50 take-home after all fees. With default fees, you would need approximately $16.30 in fees to net $12.50 — meaning your price needs to be roughly $20.30 (where $16.30 is 6.5% + 3% + fixed fees). Price sensitivity on Etsy is high because buyers compare across sellers easily, so it is often more practical to reduce fees by optimizing your product category, reducing shipping costs, or driving direct traffic to bypass off-site ads, rather than simply raising prices.

What fees do I pay when I use Etsy's shipping labels?

When you buy shipping labels through Etsy, you pay the carrier rate (USPS, UPS, FedEx) at Etsy's negotiated discount — typically 5–30% below retail counter rates. This is a separate cost from Etsy's seller fees and is not deducted from your sale proceeds. You pay for shipping labels separately from your shop balance. Etsy does not take a commission on shipping label purchases — it makes money on the carrier discount. If you set up your own shipping carrier accounts and pass the full shipping cost to the buyer (buyer-pays-shipping model), the shipping fee is included in the total order value and Etsy's transaction and payment processing fees apply to the shipping amount as well.

Does Etsy refund fees on cancelled orders?

Etsy does not refund transaction fees, listing fees, or payment processing fees on cancelled or refunded orders. This is a significant risk for custom orders where the buyer cancels or requests a refund after the work is done. For custom orders, some sellers require payment upfront and charge a cancellation fee equal to the Etsy fees incurred, or build the fees into the item price and require non-refundable deposits. When you accept a custom order on Etsy, you are accepting the terms that fees are non-refundable regardless of whether the transaction completes or is reversed.

What is the difference between gross revenue and net profit on Etsy?

Gross revenue is your total sales dollars before any costs. Net profit is what remains after Etsy fees AND your cost of goods sold (materials, packaging, labor). Many Etsy sellers set prices based on Etsy fees alone and are surprised to find their net margin is much lower after accounting for all costs. A common rule of thumb for handmade sellers: if your Etsy fees total approximately 15% of gross (including off-site ads), your cost of goods should be no more than 25% of gross to achieve a 40% net margin before overhead. This leaves room for packaging, labor, and business overhead while still generating real profit.
