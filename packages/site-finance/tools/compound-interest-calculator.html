How compound interest works

Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest, not just your principal. Over long time horizons this creates exponential rather than linear growth. Albert Einstein allegedly called it the eighth wonder of the world — whether or not he said it, the mathematics are undeniable: at 7% annual return, your money doubles roughly every 10 years.

The inflation adjustment converts nominal returns (what you see in the account) to real returns (what you can actually buy). At 7% return and 3% inflation, your real annual return is approximately 4%, and your balance after 20 years is worth significantly less in today's purchasing power than the nominal figure suggests.
