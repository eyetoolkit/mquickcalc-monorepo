Where an FBA sale's money goes

Amazon takes a referral fee (a % of the sale, ~15% across many categories) plus an FBA fulfillment fee for picking, packing and shipping. Your own costs are the product cost and sending inventory into the warehouse. What's left before Amazon's payment settlements is your profit.

How Amazon FBA fees work

When you sell through Amazon FBA, Amazon charges fees at multiple stages of the sale. Understanding each one is essential before sourcing inventory, because the difference between a profitable product and a losing one often comes down to fee miscalculation. There are four major cost categories: referral fees, FBA fulfillment fees, storage fees, and inbound shipping costs. This calculator handles the first three variable costs; you enter your own inbound shipping cost.

Referral fees are Amazon's commission on each sale and are charged as a percentage of the selling price. Most categories fall in the 15% range, but the rate varies significantly: apparel is 17%, consumer electronics is 8%, and most other categories fall between 12% and 15%. The fee is applied to the sale price before Amazon deducts anything else, so a $30 item in the 15% category costs you $4.50 in referral fees before fulfillment fees. Referral fees changed in 2024 with Amazon's January fee update — always verify the current rate for your specific category on Amazon's fee schedule page.

FBA fulfillment fees cover the cost of Amazon picking your item from the warehouse, packing it, and shipping it to the customer. These fees are charged per unit and are determined by the size tier and weight of your product. Standard-size items are further divided into six tiers based on weight: the first tier (under 1 oz) costs around $3.20, while the heaviest standard tier (over 2 lb) costs around $7.20 per unit. Large bulky items cost more. These fees increased significantly in 2024 and have increased nearly every year since 2020. Experienced sellers always model their numbers with the highest possible fee tier before committing to a product.

Storage fees are charged monthly for the space your inventory occupies in Amazon's warehouse. The rate is $0.78 per cubic foot for standard-size items from January to September, and rises to $2.40 per cubic foot from October to December to reflect the increased demand for warehouse space during the holiday season. For a typical shoebox-sized item, monthly storage might cost $0.20-$0.30 per unit, but this is often omitted in quick profitability calculations. If your inventory sits for months, storage fees can erode margins significantly.

Removal and disposal fees apply when you want to pull slow-moving inventory out of Amazon's warehouse or dispose of it. Amazon charges a per-unit removal fee that is often higher than your item's value — for this reason, many sellers prefer to liquidate slow inventory at salvage value rather than pay full removal fees. FBA reimbursement rates for damaged or lost inventory are another variable: Amazon's reimbursement rate for lost items is generally good but not perfect, and some sellers build in a small buffer for the occasional unrecoverable unit.

Understanding your profit breakdown

Amazon FBA profitability is best understood as a chain of deductions from the selling price. Working backwards from $29.99: if Amazon's referral fee is 15% ($4.50) and the FBA fulfillment fee is $4.50, Amazon takes $9.00 total. Your product cost and inbound shipping might total $9.50, leaving you $29.99 − $9.00 − $9.50 = $11.49 before accounting for storage, refunds, advertising, or your own time. Your actual net margin is that $11.49 minus all remaining costs.

ROI (Return on Investment) tells a different story than margin. If you invest $9.50 to acquire one unit and earn $11.49 profit per unit, your ROI per unit is $11.49 ÷ $9.50 = 121%. But this ignores the time your capital is tied up — if that unit sits in Amazon's warehouse for 90 days before selling, your annualized return is much lower than it appears. Many new sellers are attracted to high per-unit margins but fail to account for the turnover rate of their capital.

Breakeven analysis is the most important calculation before sourcing. Your breakeven selling price is your total cost per unit divided by (1 minus the referral fee percentage), with the fulfillment fee added on top. For a product with $9.50 in product and inbound costs, at a 15% referral fee and $4.50 fulfillment fee, your breakeven is ($9.50 + $4.50) ÷ (1 − 0.15) = $16.47. If comparable products are selling for $19.99, your gross margin before storage and advertising is $3.52 per unit — enough to be viable if turnover is fast, but thin if the product sits.

How to improve FBA margins

The most effective way to improve FBA margins is to reduce product and shipping costs before optimizing anything else. A $1 reduction in your product cost flows directly to your bottom line at 100% — unlike reducing referral fees, which you cannot control. This is why experienced sellers spend as much time on supplier negotiation as they do on product research. Moving from a $9 to an $8 product cost on a $29.99 item with $4.50 fulfillment and 15% referral improves profit from $11.49 to $12.49 per unit — an 8.7% improvement in profitability without selling a single additional unit.

Reducing product size and weight tier is the second most powerful lever. Because FBA fulfillment fees are tiered by weight and dimensions, moving from a 1 lb 2 oz item to a 14 oz item can reduce your fulfillment fee by $0.50-$1.00 per unit. Over 1,000 units per month, that is $500-$1,000 in monthly savings. Packaging redesign — reducing void fill, using flatter boxes, or switching to poly bags where allowed — can shift your size tier and dramatically reduce fulfillment costs.

Shipping mode matters more than many new sellers realize. Air shipping costs 3-5x more than sea freight per unit but ties up less capital for less time. For a slow-moving product, sea freight is almost always better. For a fast-moving product where restocking speed matters, a hybrid approach (sea to a 3PL, then Amazon FBM or express air to Amazon) can optimize both cost and availability. Calculating your true cost per unit including shipping mode and storage time is the difference between hobby sellers and professional operators.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Amazon FBA actually cost in total?

For a typical $20-$50 product in the general category, Amazon's total fees — referral fee plus fulfillment fee — typically consume 25-35% of the selling price. On a $29.99 item with a 15% referral fee and $4.50 fulfillment, Amazon takes $9.00 (30%). Your remaining $20.99 must cover your product cost, inbound shipping, storage, advertising, and still leave a profit. Many first-time sellers are surprised to find that after Amazon's deductions, their margin per unit is too thin to sustain the business profitably — running this calculation before sourcing is the single most important risk management step in FBA.

What is a good FBA profit margin?

Experienced FBA sellers typically target 25-35% net margin after all costs. This sounds high, but FBA involves real risks: inventory that does not sell and requires removal, Amazon reimbursements that are not always complete, seasonal demand swings, and competitor price wars. A 25% net margin gives you a buffer for these normal course-of-business costs. If you are targeting less than 20% net margin, the business may survive in good conditions but struggle when costs increase — and Amazon raises fees nearly every year. ROI is a better metric than margin: a lower-margin, faster-turning product can outperform a high-margin slow-mover.

Does this calculator include storage fees?

No — this calculator focuses on per-unit variable costs: referral fees, fulfillment fees, product cost, and inbound shipping. Storage fees are time-dependent (charged monthly) and vary by how long your inventory sits. To account for storage, estimate your monthly storage cost as: cubic feet used × $0.78 (Jan-Sep) or $2.40 (Oct-Dec). For a shoebox-sized item occupying roughly 0.1 cubic feet, monthly storage is about $0.08-$0.24 per unit. Storage on slow-moving inventory can quietly consume your margins — calculate your estimated storage cost per unit based on your expected days-to-sell before committing to a product.

What referral fee does my category use?

Referral fees vary by category and range from 8% to 17%. The most common rate is 15% for general merchandise. Apparel is 17%, consumer electronics is 8%, books are 15%, and toys are 8% in the off-season (January-August). Category-level referral fees are listed on Amazon's official fee schedule page, which Amazon updates at least annually. Using the wrong referral fee percentage in this calculator will give you incorrect results — always verify your specific category's rate. If you sell across multiple categories, each may carry a different referral fee rate.

How do I account for Amazon refunds?

Refunds in FBA are handled by Amazon and include a refund administration fee on top of the per-unit referral fee. When an item is returned, Amazon refunds the customer the sale price but deducts the original referral fee (partially or fully returned depending on the reason) and charges a $0.00-$2.00 refund administration fee. As a rule of thumb, budget for a 5-15% refund rate on your product category — higher for clothing, lower for electronics. If your refund rate exceeds 15%, it may indicate a product quality or listing accuracy issue that is worth investigating before it damages your account health.

Is FBA still profitable in 2024-2025?

FBA is still profitable for sellers who choose the right products and manage their costs carefully. Amazon's fee increases in 2024 significantly compressed margins across many categories, and sellers who were profitable at thin margins were hit hardest. The products that continue to work well in FBA tend to have either high average selling prices (above $35) where the percentage fees are more manageable, or very low product costs that absorb the flat fulfillment fees. Commoditized products competing primarily on price are increasingly difficult to sell through FBA. Private label brands with meaningful differentiation, bundling, or proprietary designs continue to find profitable niches.

What's the difference between FBA and FBM for profitability?

FBM (Fulfillment by Merchant) means you store, pack, and ship each order yourself. The FBM seller avoids Amazon's fulfillment fee (typically $4-$9 per unit for FBA) but pays for their own warehouse space, labor, packing materials, shipping labels, and handling. For most sellers, FBM makes economic sense only for large/bulky items where Amazon's dimensional weight pricing is unfavorable, or for slow-moving products where the storage fee savings with a personal warehouse exceed the labor cost. Hybrid approaches — using FBA for your best sellers and FBM for slower items — are common among experienced operators.
