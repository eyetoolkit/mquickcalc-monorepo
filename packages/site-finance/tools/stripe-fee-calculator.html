How Stripe pricing works

Standard card payments cost a percentage plus a flat amount (commonly 2.9% + $0.30 in the US). The bigger the charge, the closer the effective rate gets to the headline percentage — which is why a fixed fee matters most on small-ticket sales.

How Stripe fees work

Stripe's standard rate is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful card charge. This applies to most US and European businesses using Stripe Checkout or custom payment forms. The flat 30¢ component dominates small transactions — a $1 coffee charge loses about 17% to Stripe.

Stripe offers discounted rates for volume merchants (2.7% + 20¢ for 6-figure+ revenue), non-profits (2.2% + 30¢), and ACH bank transfers (0.8% with a $5 cap). Businesses processing mostly small payments should consider ACH or PayPal Here to reduce per-transaction overhead.

International cards incur an additional 1% fee, and currency conversion adds another 1%. Refunds and disputes still incur the original processing fee — Stripe only waives fees on reversed charges.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Stripe fee for card payments?

In the US it's usually 2.9% + $0.30 per successful card charge. Fees differ for international cards, invoicing, recurring or other products.

How do I avoid a flat fee hit on small sales?

Because of the $0.30 fixed part, the effective rate is highest on small amounts. Pricing bundles or minimums can smooth that out, or use the "To receive" line to protect your target net.
